Smart Travel: Proven Travel Tips

Look forward to the journey, not just the destination.

Whether by car, plane, or ship – discover proven strategies and preventive tips to support you with motion sickness (kinetosis) and flight anxiety. Learn how to actively support your wellbeing while traveling and where classic home remedies reach their limits. This is exactly where digital tools step in to serve as your smart companion in any situation.

Tips for the Road: What to do about travel uneasiness in the car?

  • Ideally, drive yourself: As a driver, you can anticipate movements. This allows the brain to perfectly coordinate vision and the sense of balance.
  • Sit in the passenger seat: The view of the horizon is best here. This provides the brain with the necessary visual orientation for the movement.
  • Avoid reading on a screen: Staring at static texts massively increases the sensory conflict between your eyes and the balance organ.
  • Get some fresh air: Fresh, cool air stimulates the facial nerve (trigeminal nerve), which can often immediately dampen emerging discomfort.

Tips for Sea Voyages: Supporting wellbeing on a ship

  • Head for the middle of the ship: In the middle of the ship (the pivot point), the swaying is minimal, which significantly relieves the inner ear.
  • Get out into the fresh air: Looking at the stable horizon helps the brain visually categorize the felt wave movements.
  • Lie down if necessary: When lying down, the body doesn't have to balance against gravity. This calms the sensors in the balance organ (otoliths).

Tips for Relaxed Flying: Reducing flight anxiety and stress

  • Choose a seat over the wings: The aircraft's center of gravity is located over the wings. This is where you feel turbulence the least.
  • Direct the air vent at your face: Cool air on your face activates cold stimuli (thermal receptors) that can help reduce queasiness and sweating.
  • Use noise-canceling headphones: Constant noise is stress for the brain. Silence reduces the sensory overload and significantly helps you relax.

Before the Journey: Preventing and building resilience

  • Travel with a light stomach: A completely empty stomach is often more sensitive. A small, easily digestible meal (or some fresh ginger) before departure helps digestion and soothes gently.
  • Prepare yourself calmly: A well-rested body is more resilient. Lack of sleep enormously lowers the tolerance threshold for motion sickness.
