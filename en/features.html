Immediate support for seasickness

The artificial horizon

Seasickness is caused by a sensory conflict: The eye reports a standstill – for example, in a windowless inner cabin of a ship – while the balance organ simultaneously feels the movement. Some ship passengers currently use a half-filled water bottle on the table below deck to make the ship's movement visible to the eye again. The artificial horizon in the app is essentially the modern, digital translation of this principle directly on the display. It gently balances the conflict by making the real movement synchronously visible. Ideally, use this method on a ship below deck.

Why this helps: The display of an earth-referenced line provides the brain with the missing visual movement information. On the screen, you see the bow of a ship and the sea with a mountain range on the horizon. While the bow remains rigid, the background image tilts exactly in sync with the actual swaying based on your smartphone's sensor data. Your brain thus gets the missing visual confirmation of the movement back, which gently soothes the queasy feeling in your stomach.

Scientific Background & Sources

Background: The display of an earth-referenced line provides the brain with the missing visual movement information (inertial cues). This resolves the sensory conflict and prevents the error message in the brain that leads to travel discomfort.

Source: Bos, J. E., et al. (2005/2012). TNO Human Factors / Vrije Universiteit.

Calming for fear of flying & stress

4-2-6 paced breathing

Find your inner balance again. Use paced breathing when you urgently need an anchor on a plane or bus. It is an ideal companion for motion sickness and rising fear of flying, as it immediately supports your wellbeing. Haptic signals guide you completely blindly through the rhythm: inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 2 seconds, exhale for 6 seconds.

Why this helps: The conscious and prolonged exhalation specifically activates your body's resting nerve. This signals safety to the brain, gently winds down acute stress reactions and feelings of panic, and noticeably dampens the urge to vomit.

Scientific Background & Sources

Background: Controlled, slowed breathing (paced breathing) stimulates the vagus nerve and increases heart rate variability (HRV). This significantly dampens physical arousal and has proven effective in studies as support for restless travel and in stressful situations.

Source: Yen Pik Sang et al. (2003); Stromberg et al. (2015); Zaccaro et al. (2018) on anxiety regulation.

Preventive training for the journey

Mental rotation

Prepare yourself weeks before your vacation so the trip to your destination starts relaxed. A trained spatial imagination helps the brain process spatial and movement data on the journey more efficiently. IMPORTANT: This is purely a preventive training. Practice calmly for about 15 minutes a day before the trip. The application: Decide in the interactive game whether the right 3D figure is identical (only rotated) or mirrored. You have one joker per level.

Why this helps: Such spatial vision training trains the brain to resolve conflicting signals and movements much faster. This can significantly lower individual susceptibility to travel discomfort in advance.

Scientific Background & Sources

Background: Research by the University of Warwick (2021) showed that a two-week spatial vision training (visuospatial training) can reduce susceptibility to travel discomfort by over 50%. A trained spatial imagination helps the brain process movement data more efficiently.

Source: Smyth, J. et al. (2021). University of Warwick / Applied Ergonomics.

Further Methods & Knowledge

For long-term habituation, preparation, and your wellbeing on the go.

P6 Acupressure

The gentle massage of the Nei-Kuan point sends soothing signals to the stomach. Use this manual technique right at the first signs of motion sickness or preventively shortly before the journey begins.

Why this helps: The massage of this point is proven to noticeably reduce the occurrence of travel discomfort and support the general wellbeing of the stomach.

In the app: Viativity provides a detailed guide on exactly how to find the point on your wrist, as well as an interactive timer for the perfect massage duration.
Scientific Background & Sources

Background: Systematic reviews (Cochrane Reviews) confirm that the stimulation of the P6 point can significantly reduce the occurrence of a queasy feeling and discomfort. The effect on the general wellbeing of the stomach is comprehensively proven in studies, entirely without side effects.

Source: Lee A, Fan LT. (2009/2015). Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.

Body Stability

Discomfort while traveling is often preceded by an insecure stance. With this exercise, you train your core stability on solid ground – purely via audio and haptic feedback with closed eyes.

Why this helps: The interactive training improves your posture control. Higher physical stability (less swaying) leads to a much lower susceptibility to travel discomfort.

In the app: You press the smartphone slightly against your breastbone and close your eyes. Viativity gives you real-time feedback on your swaying via gentle vibrations, helping you learn to find your inner center.
Scientific Background & Sources

Background: Biofeedback training improves posture control. Studies (Postural Instability Theory) show that higher stability (less swaying) directly correlates with a higher resistance to travel discomfort.

Source: Riccio & Stoffregen (1991); Stoffregen et al. (2010).

100Hz Tone Modulation

Specific acoustic frequencies can gently influence the balance system via the inner ear. Listen to the tone preventively shortly before your trip or as support at the first signs of discomfort.

Why this helps: The fine vibration stimulates the sensory cells in the ear, improves balance, and thus reduces the sensory conflict in the brain, actively counteracting the queasy feeling.

In the app: A 100Hz generator delivers the pure tone directly to your own headphones – available anytime and completely offline.
Scientific Background & Sources

Background: Current research from Nagoya University (2025) shows that a 100Hz vibration stimulates the sensory cells in the ear (otolith organs) and improves vestibular balance. This reduces the sensory conflict in the brain and significantly lowers susceptibility to travel discomfort.

Source: Kagawa, T., Kato, M. et al. (2025). Nagoya University Graduate School of Medicine.

Focus

This exercise requires high visual concentration. It is perfectly suited as preventive training in advance or for gentle habituation directly during the journey.

Why this helps: Constantly fixating on a small dot binds your attention, fades out disturbing movement stimuli, and thus mitigates the conflict between your eyes and your sense of balance.

In the app: An interactive game requires your full concentration to keep a small circle exactly in the center, effectively distracting you from emerging discomfort.
Scientific Background & Sources

Background: Fixating on a stable point suppresses the reflex that links eyes and balance (vestibulo-ocular reflex). This mitigates the sensory conflict, which is considered in science to be the main cause of travel discomfort.

Source: Reason, J. T., & Brand, J. J. (1975); Bos (2011).
